01 / About

A researcher at heart.
A builder by curiosity.

I work across field experiments, laboratory measurements and data analysis to understand agricultural systems and improve their environmental performance.

I am a Postdoctoral Researcher at Teagasc Johnstown Castle, working on biochar in agriculture. My current research connects perennial biomass, manure management and soil application to investigate carbon storage, nutrient recycling and emissions mitigation.

I hold a PhD in Biosystems & Food Engineering from University College Dublin, completed through a Teagasc Walsh Fellowship. My doctoral research examined ammonia and methane emissions from dairy housing and stored manure, alongside the validation of low-cost gas sensors.

Alongside my research, I am developing practical cybersecurity skills through certification, independent projects and hands-on learning. I am particularly interested in how environmental research and cybersecurity can contribute to more resilient agricultural systems.

Understanding how systems work, measuring what matters, and turning evidence into practical improvements.

Doctoral research / Measurement

Gas sensor validation in dairy housing

Assessing low-cost sensors against reference measurements in naturally ventilated dairy buildings.

Gasera ONESensor networksCO₂ · NH₃ · CH₄
Explore the methods

Compared a low-cost wireless sensor network with a reference gas analyser under real barn conditions, examining gas concentration agreement and the challenges of measuring emissions in naturally ventilated housing.

Doctoral research / Evidence synthesis

Diet and emissions from stored cattle manure

Examining how forage-to-concentrate ratio, crude protein and feed additives influence emissions during manure storage.

Systematic reviewMeta-analysismetafor
Explore the analysis

Synthesised experimental evidence using meta-analysis in R to evaluate treatment effects, between-study heterogeneity and the influence of dietary moderators on manure emissions.
